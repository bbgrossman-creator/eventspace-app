# The EventCore Product Constitution

*The middle document. The Atlas Constitution governs what is true. The Interaction Constitution governs how it is met. This governs what the product is.*

---

## Article 1 · Purpose

**PC‑1.1** EventCore exists to make a catering business run from a single shared truth instead of from many disagreeing copies of it.

**PC‑1.2** The operational problem it solves is not scheduling, not documents, and not communication. It is **divergence**. A count changes on Wednesday; the kitchen works from Monday's sheet, the warehouse from Tuesday's, the coordinator from a phone call nobody wrote down. Every person is competent, every person is working hard, and every person is working from a different truth. Nearly every expensive failure in catering is a version-control failure wearing an apron.

**PC‑1.3** EventCore's purpose is therefore to hold **one continuous identity** for each engagement, from the first phone call to final settlement, and to let each operator work on it through the part that concerns them — without ever transcribing, re-entering, or reconciling.

**PC‑1.4** Its second purpose is to make institutional knowledge survive the people who hold it. What a company knows about a venue's loading dock, a supplier's reliability, or how a dish travels currently lives in heads and leaves at 6pm. EventCore's purpose includes **accumulating that knowledge deliberately** and putting it in front of whoever needs it next.

**PC‑1.5** Its third purpose is to answer, at any moment and for any operator, two questions: *what is in the way*, and *what should I do next*. Not by maintaining a list somebody types, but as a **consequence of what has actually been recorded**.

**PC‑1.6** What EventCore is **not** for: replacing judgment, scoring people, predicting outcomes, or automating decisions that belong to an operator. It removes the work of reconciliation so that human attention goes where it is worth spending.

## Article 2 · Operators

Nine canonical operators. Each owns something no one else may author.

**PC‑2.1** *Owns* means the workspace is the sole authority for that class of fact. *Authors* means it records them. *Observes* means it reads facts others own. *Never changes* means the product must make the act impossible, not merely discouraged.

### Sales
- **Owns** — the client relationship, the commercial pipeline, the date hold.
- **Authors** — inquiry facts, holds and their release, the decision to propose, the record of acceptance.
- **Observes** — availability and capacity, composed feasibility, Library knowledge, client history.
- **Never changes** — any operational fact. Sales cannot alter a count, a roster, a production plan, or a cost.

### Event Management
- **Owns** — coordination. The run of show, the venue and vendor liaison, compliance obligations, the count ceremony, the debrief.
- **Authors** — the timeline, coordination facts, compliance obligations and their discharge, amendments (through Proposal Studio), the final count.
- **Observes** — every workspace's state and its grounds. It composes feasibility from facts others author.
- **Never changes** — another workspace's execution facts. It may see that Kitchen is blocked; it may not unblock it. **Accountability without authorship** is the whole of the coordinator's role.

### Kitchen
- **Owns** — production, and everything that becomes food.
- **Authors** — prepped, cooked, packed, released; purchasing and receiving; food cost; allergen handling.
- **Observes** — counts, the accepted menu, dietary and religious constraints, release times.
- **Never changes** — price, rentals, rosters.

### Warehouse
- **Owns** — everything that is not food.
- **Authors** — receipt, pull, stage, load, return; shortages and damages.
- **Observes** — the accepted equipment requirement, counts, load times.
- **Never changes** — food, price, labour.

### Setup
- **Owns** — the room.
- **Authors** — access, load-in, layout, dress, service-ready, breakdown, load-out.
- **Observes** — the accepted layout, venue constraints, the timeline.
- **Never changes** — anything that arrives; it places what others send.

### Transport
- **Owns** — movement.
- **Authors** — routes, loads, departures, arrivals, returns.
- **Observes** — what must move, when it must arrive, where it must return.
- **Never changes** — the contents of what it carries.

### Staffing
- **Owns** — labour, in every category: culinary, service, captains, bartenders, drivers, setup crew.
- **Authors** — forecast, roster, confirmations, briefings, on-site presence, time worked.
- **Observes** — counts, service style, venue, timeline.
- **Never changes** — production, price, equipment.

### Money
- **Owns** — the money.
- **Authors** — invoices, receipts, payables, cost reconciliation, settlement of financial obligations.
- **Observes** — the accepted promise, every amendment, actuals from every workspace.
- **Never changes** — any operational fact. Money may not adjust a count to make an invoice agree.

### Owner
- **Owns** — nothing operational.
- **Authors** — **intent only**: targets, thresholds, policy, priorities.
- **Observes** — everything, aggregated.
- **Never changes** — any canonical fact, and never produces an operational verdict. If the owner needs an answer no workspace gives, the missing fact is added to the workspace responsible for it.

**PC‑2.2** These nine are complete for the operating business. Proposal Studio is not among them because it is a mode, not an operator (Article 5). The Book and the Library are places, not operators (Articles 6 and 8).

## Article 3 · Workspaces

Each workspace is a **station**: a place where a particular operator does a particular kind of work on a shared subject. It is not a view, not a report, and not a module.

**PC‑3.1** For each: why it exists, what enters, what leaves, and what makes it complete.

| Workspace | Exists because | Enters | Leaves | Complete when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales** | somebody must decide whether we take this, and hold the relationship while the client decides | an inquiry, and a date question | an accepted promise — or a recorded loss with its reason | the file is accepted or lost, and either way the reason is on record |
| **Event Management** | somebody must be accountable for the event actually happening | an accepted promise | a coordinated event, and afterwards a debrief | every workspace has what it needs, the run of show is settled, and the debrief is harvested |
| **Kitchen** | food must be produced | counts, menu, restrictions, release times | food, released and packed | everything on the menu has been produced and released for its service |
| **Warehouse** | everything that is not food must be gathered, sent and recovered | an equipment requirement | a loaded truck, and later a reconciled return | everything sent has come back or been accounted for |
| **Setup** | a venue must become the promised room | a layout, an access window, delivered goods | a service-ready room, and later a cleared one | the room matches what was promised, and afterwards is left as found |
| **Transport** | things and people must be somewhere else | a load and a time | arrival, and return | everything has arrived and returned |
| **Staffing** | events are run by people, and people must be found, confirmed and briefed | a labour requirement | a confirmed, briefed crew on site | every role is filled, confirmed, briefed and present |
| **Money** | the business must be paid, and must know what it cost | an accepted promise and its amendments | a settled account | invoiced, collected, payables closed, cost reconciled |
| **Owner** | somebody must see the business rather than the event | everything, already authored | judgment and intent | never — it is continuous |

**PC‑3.2 · Workspaces sleep; they never disappear.** Kitchen exists during an inquiry, quiet, saying that nothing is required of it yet. The structure of the product never changes shape as an event progresses, because operators must learn it once.

**PC‑3.3 · A workspace shows only what its operator authors or must act on.** Kitchen never shows price. Sales never shows pull lists.

**PC‑3.4 · A workspace may display another's state but never offers to change it.**

**PC‑3.5 · Each workspace has its own clock.** Kitchen works backward from service; Sales forward to a decision date; Setup to a venue's access window; Money to payment terms. The event date is context, not the frame.

**PC‑3.6 · No workspace owns the Event File.** Every one of them is complete on its own terms and partial with respect to the event.

## Article 4 · The Event File

**PC‑4.1** The Event File is the complete record of one promise to one client, from first contact to final settlement. It is the product's central object and the only identity an operator ever holds.

**PC‑4.2** It begins at **inquiry** — before anything is sold, before anything is certain. It ends at **settlement** — after the last obligation is discharged.

**PC‑4.3 · Acceptance changes state; it never creates a second object.** The proposal does not become an event; the file becomes accepted. Operational workspaces activate over the same identity. The client experienced one continuous relationship, and the file has the same shape as their experience.

**PC‑4.4 · Services sit beneath the file.** A simcha may carry a Friday dinner, a Shabbos lunch and a Sunday brunch. Commercial work — Sales, the promise, the agreement, the account — operates at file grain. Operational work — Kitchen, Warehouse, Setup, Transport, Staffing — may operate at service grain. A service has no independent client, no proposal, and no commercial identity. **It is not a second Event File.**

**PC‑4.5 · Why one identity matters.** Because every alternative requires transcription, and every transcription is a place where truth forks. One identity is what makes it possible for the count the client agreed to be the count the kitchen cooks to, the count the truck loads for, and the count the invoice bills.

**PC‑4.6 · Every workspace sees the same file differently.** Kitchen sees production. Money sees obligation. Setup sees a room. None of them is looking at a copy, an export, or a summary. They are looking at the same file through the part that concerns them.

**PC‑4.7 · The operator never experiences multiple event objects.** There is no "proposal record" that becomes an "event record" that spawns a "job." There is one file whose state changes and whose workspaces wake up.

**PC‑4.8 · Above the file sits the client account.** History, preferences, allergies, standing terms, credit and payment behaviour, and the relationship itself. A client with a standing weekly order has one account and many files. The account is context; the file is the unit of work.

## Article 5 · Proposal Studio

**PC‑5.1 · What it is.** The sole authoring environment for the Event File's promise. Every workspace executes *against* the promise; Proposal Studio is the only place the promise itself is composed or changed. It is a **mode of the file**, not a workspace and not an operator.

**PC‑5.2 · Two modes of the file.** *Authoring* — deciding what we will promise. *Operating* — doing something about what we promised. Sales lives at the boundary and uses both.

**PC‑5.3 · Tracks, not a sequence.** The promise is composed in parallel tracks — menu, design, venue and layout, service, staffing, equipment, timeline, pricing, terms and documents. Each has its own state and may be approved independently. **No fixed order is constitutional.** A kosher caterer may lead with menu; a design-led firm with concept; a venue-constrained event with layout. The product must not imply a sequence it does not have.

**PC‑5.4 · Options are decisions, not text.** Either/or, add-on, tiered, and count-flexed choices are decisions the client makes, and until made, the operational consequence is genuinely unknown. The Studio must be able to say what remains undecided and what it is worth.

**PC‑5.5 · Live Preview is continuous.** The authoring face is structured and cost-aware; the client face is a single coherent document. There is no gap between what is being written and what will be read.

**PC‑5.6 · Versioning and comparison.** Every version survives, and *what changed* is a first-class answer, not a diff of two documents.

**PC‑5.7 · Amendments.** After acceptance the same tools carry different gravity: an amendment changes something a client agreed to. It must show its own consequence — what changes, what it costs, who must approve, which workspaces are affected — **before** it is made. A change two weeks out and the same change two days out are different acts.

**PC‑5.8 · The promise is the specification.** On acceptance, promised elements become operational requirements directly. The menu becomes production; the equipment becomes a pull; the layout becomes a setup plan; the staffing line becomes a roster requirement. **Nothing is ever transcribed.** This is the property from which most of EventCore's value follows, and it is the acid test for the Studio: if something can be promised but cannot become an operational requirement, it is decoration and should not be proposable.

**PC‑5.9 · Feasibility is composed, never scored.** The Studio may draft freely but may not silently classify an operationally impossible promise as cleared. Before send or acceptance, feasibility is composed from facts authored by the workspaces responsible for them — Kitchen for production capacity, Staffing for labour, Warehouse for equipment, Setup for venue and installation, Transport for movement. **Event Management composes and displays it; it authors none of it.** There is no score, and none is computed anywhere but from those facts.

**PC‑5.10 · Unresolved feasibility requires a ceremony, not an override.** A promise may proceed with unresolved feasibility only through an explicit authorised exception that records the unresolved condition, the risk, the approving authority, the affected scope, and the required follow-up.

**PC‑5.11 · Composition and instantiation.** The Studio composes from the Library: it instantiates Compositions and knowledge assets into the promise. **Instantiation copies knowledge and never copies institutional history** — a station pulled today arrives as today's doctrine, not carrying the improvisation someone made in 2023 when a supplier failed.

## Article 6 · Knowledge

**PC‑6.1** The Knowledge Library is the institution's reusable memory. It is what a company knows, held so that it outlives the people who learned it.

**PC‑6.2 · Two species of knowledge, governed differently.**

- **Doctrine** — what we *intend*. Recipes, yields, packages, layouts, service standards, labour ratios, pricing doctrine, packing and transport method, compliance requirements. Deliberately authored and governed. **Copied at instantiation and frozen**, so a promise is stable and a later doctrine change never silently rewrites a signed agreement.
- **Experience** — what we *learned*. This venue's dock closes at four. This supplier is late. This family has a coeliac guest. Accumulated rather than authored. **Referenced live, never copied**, so the newest lesson always reaches the person about to need it.

**PC‑6.3 · Copy doctrine; reference experience.** This single distinction makes the Library both stable and alive.

**PC‑6.4 · Compositions.** A Composition is the institution's **approved reusable experience**, composed from governed knowledge assets — not a folder of items but a curated, approved whole that can be instantiated as one act. Compositions are governed: they are approved, versioned, and retirable.

**PC‑6.5 · Historical extraction.** A past event is a legitimate source of knowledge. What was actually done — the menu as served, the layout as built, the crew as staffed — may be **extracted** from a completed file as a candidate.

**PC‑6.6 · Promotion is a governance act.** An extracted candidate is not knowledge until it is **promoted** into a governed Composition by someone with the authority to do so. Extraction observes; promotion decides. Without this boundary the Library would fill with everything that ever happened, including the mistakes.

**PC‑6.7 · The Library must learn, and learning needs a ritual.** Experience does not accumulate by itself. The **debrief** is a designed step in the lifecycle — short, structured, station-scoped: what surprised you, what would you tell the next crew, what should we never do here again. Its output is attributed and dated. A ritual nobody performs is not a feature, so the product must make the debrief the path of least resistance.

**PC‑6.8 · Knowledge is used from inside the flow.** You compose a promise from the Library, build a crew brief from it, pack a kit from it. It is the company's memory, not a settings screen.

**PC‑6.9 · A company's real asset is its accumulated operational memory.** Most of it currently walks out of the building every evening. Capturing it is not a feature of EventCore; it is one of its purposes.

## Article 7 · Operational execution

*The operator experience after acceptance.*

**PC‑7.1 · Acceptance is a handoff, not a status change.** The file announces itself to the workspaces that now have work. Nobody is expected to notice a field changed.

**PC‑7.2 · Each workspace receives requirements, not instructions.** Kitchen receives what must be produced; Staffing what must be crewed; Warehouse what must be sent. **How** is theirs.

**PC‑7.3 · Work is described by what is in the way.** No workspace maintains a checklist. Each shows what is outstanding and, of that, what is impeded — with the reason and the responsible party attached. A number without its grounds is not an answer.

**PC‑7.4 · One next action, with its reason.** Every operator, in every workspace, is offered a single next action phrased as an instruction, carrying the ground that selected it. Never a ranked list of everything.

**PC‑7.5 · Ready means unimpeded, not complete.** A department with twelve outstanding tasks and nothing standing in their way is ready. Completion is a different axis, and the two are never merged.

**PC‑7.6 · Lawful states are visible, never hidden.** Work nobody owns yet is lawful and must be seen. The product surfaces it as a gap to be closed, never as an error and never silently.

**PC‑7.7 · The count is the spine, and there are four of them.** Contracted, guaranteed, final, actual — each real, each different, and confusing them is how caterers lose money invisibly. The **final count is a ceremony**, not a field: it shows its blast radius across every workspace before it commits, and every workspace receives it as an announced change.

**PC‑7.8 · Change carries its consequence.** Any amendment, any count movement, any substitution shows what it affects before it is made.

**PC‑7.9 · "What changed since I last looked" is a first-class surface.** In every workspace, scoped to what that operator cares about, since *that person* last looked. It is the direct remedy for PC‑1.2 and the most valuable thing the product can offer once one file holds the truth.

**PC‑7.10 · Refusals speak in the business's voice.** When the system declines an act, it names the rule in the operator's language, verbatim. It never shows a code, and never says merely that something failed.

**PC‑7.11 · Late completion clears the impediment; accountability persists.** Work completed after its window is no longer in the way — the required outcome exists. A non-impeding accountability note remains until reviewed. The system does not manufacture blockage to express disapproval.

**PC‑7.12 · Completion is not settlement.** The last guest leaving ends the event, not the file. Settlement follows only when services are completed, breakdown done, owned and rented items returned, shortages and damages reconciled, vendor obligations closed, amendments closed, final payment resolved, and cost reconciled. **The file closes when its obligations are discharged, not when the party ends.**

## Article 8 · The Day axis

**PC‑8.1 · Why it exists.** Because a chef does not work on events. A chef works on a Tuesday: four events, one kitchen, one team, one prep list. A van serves a day, not a file. A warehouse pulls a week. Asking an operator to open five Event Files to assemble their own day is asking them to do the reconciliation EventCore exists to abolish.

**PC‑8.2 · The two axes.**
- **Vertical — the Event File.** One event across every workspace. *"How is the Bar Mitzvah?"*
- **Horizontal — the Day.** One workspace across many events over a period. *"What is Kitchen doing Tuesday?"*

**PC‑8.3 · The Day is the primary operational entrance** for Kitchen, Warehouse, Transport and Staffing. The Event File remains the primary event-centred experience and the home of identity.

**PC‑8.4 · Why it is not a second product.** Because it consumes the same canonical truth, arranged differently. It is a **projection**, not a system. Nothing is authored in the Day that is not authored in the file; nothing is stored there; nothing can be true in one and false in the other. If the Day view ever holds its own state, EventCore has rebuilt the seam it was created to remove.

**PC‑8.5 · Both axes reach the same workspace.** Kitchen entered from an event shows one thread; Kitchen entered from Tuesday shows the rope. Same station, same truth, different frame.

**PC‑8.6 · You may enter the building through any door.** A driver arrives at Transport with today's runs and walks into an event from there. Nobody is forced through a lobby of events they do not care about.

## Article 9 · Product principles

The permanent tests. A proposed feature that violates one of these does not belong in EventCore, however useful it appears.

**PC‑9.1 · Familiarity over novelty.** An operator must never feel they are learning a new application because the architecture evolved.

**PC‑9.2 · Architecture deepens beneath a stable interaction language.** Depth is what the system knows, not what it demands you look at.

**PC‑9.3 · Operators author truth.** A fact is authored by the workspace responsible for it, once, by the person who knows it.

**PC‑9.4 · Coordination never authors facts.** Seeing everything is not permission to change anything. Accountability without authorship.

**PC‑9.5 · Automation proposes; people decide.** The system may compute, order, surface and warn. It may not commit an act that belongs to a person.

**PC‑9.6 · Every workspace owns its own truth** — and only its own.

**PC‑9.7 · Every projection remains explainable.** Any number can be taken apart into the facts that produced it. An aggregate that cannot be decomposed to its grounds does not ship.

**PC‑9.8 · One promise, many consumers.** Nothing is entered twice, anywhere, ever.

**PC‑9.9 · Documents are printings, never the truth.** Proposals, BEOs, pick lists, invoices and run sheets are rendered from the file at a moment. None of them is edited, and none of them is a source.

**PC‑9.10 · Absence is stated, never implied.** Day one — when nothing has been recorded — is the normal state, not an error. The product says what it does not know.

**PC‑9.11 · The system never pretends to know.** No invented scores, no confidence it does not have, no judgment presented as fact.

**PC‑9.12 · Judgments never become canonical facts.** A composed judgment — feasibility, readiness, priority — is derived and re-derivable. It is never stored as though someone had asserted it.

**PC‑9.13 · Rollups never store.** A rollup that persists is a second source of truth wearing a summary's clothes.

**PC‑9.14 · Gates never cause.** A gate reports whether conditions are met. It does not make anything happen, and it is never the reason something is true.

**PC‑9.15 · Readiness describes; priority prescribes.** Readiness is a statement about the world and is certifiable. Priority is a statement about values and is ruled. Neither derives from the other.

**PC‑9.16 · Corrections are appended, never overwritten.** History is not edited. What was believed at a moment remains answerable.

**PC‑9.17 · Copy doctrine; reference experience.** Promises must be stable; warnings must be current.

**PC‑9.18 · Lawful states are visible.** Ownerless work, missing facts, and not-yet-due work are normal conditions and are shown as such — never hidden, never dressed as failure.

**PC‑9.19 · Every operator has their own clock.** The event date is context; each workspace's own horizon is the frame.

**PC‑9.20 · Every hesitation is a design defect.** If an operator pauses for two seconds to wonder whether they are seeing everything, whether a number is current, or whose job something is, the product has failed at that point regardless of what it does elsewhere.

## Article 10 · Boundaries

**PC‑10.1** Four documents, four jurisdictions. Material in the wrong one is a defect even when correct.

| Document | Governs | Answers | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atlas Constitution** | ontology and law | *what is true, and who may say so* | responsibility states, ownership, evidence, append-only discipline, truth versus presentation, tenancy |
| **Product Constitution** *(this)* | product philosophy | *what the product is, and what belongs in it* | operators, workspaces, the Event File, Proposal Studio, knowledge, the two axes, the principles |
| **Interaction Constitution** | interaction grammar | *how it is met* | identity band, workspace strip, scope note, next-action chip, pipeline, colour language, extension test |
| **Implementation** | mechanism | *how it is built and proved* | schema, projections, ceremonies, proofs, certification, releases |

**PC‑10.2 · Does not belong here — ontology.** What a responsibility is, what discharge requires, how evidence works, what makes a fact tenant-visible, how bitemporality behaves. This document *consumes* those definitions; it must not restate or amend them.

**PC‑10.3 · Does not belong here — interaction.** Layout, navigation, colour, typography, density, disclosure, or any screen. This document may say a workspace must show what is in the way; it may not say how.

**PC‑10.4 · Does not belong here — implementation.** Schema, projections, functions, proofs, certification, sequencing, releases. If a statement here can only be evaluated by reading code, it is misplaced.

**PC‑10.5 · Belongs here and nowhere else** — what the product is for, who its operators are, what each owns, what a workspace is and when it is complete, what the Event File means, what Proposal Studio and the Library are, how work feels after acceptance, why the Day exists, and the principles by which a proposed feature is judged.

**PC‑10.6 · The test for a proposed feature**, in order:
1. Does it serve PC‑1?
2. Does it belong to an operator who owns that truth (PC‑2)?
3. Does it violate any principle in Article 9?
4. Can the Interaction Constitution express it without a new pattern?
5. Only then: how would it be built?

A feature that fails 1–3 does not become acceptable by being easy to build, and a feature that passes 1–3 does not become unnecessary by being hard.

---

## Closing

EventCore is one file, nine operators, two axes, and a memory.

The file is the identity a business shares. The operators are the people who know things, each authoring only what they know. The axes are the two ways real work is organised — by event and by day — over one truth rather than two systems. The memory is what stops a company relearning the same lesson every year.

Everything else in this product is a consequence of those four things, and anything that cannot be traced back to one of them should be refused.
