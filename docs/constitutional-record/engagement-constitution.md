# The Engagement Constitution

*Constitutional law. It governs everything undertaken between the institution and another party.*

---

## Preamble

The Constitutional Reduction established three jurisdictions over truth: **Atlas** governs assertions about our own subjects; **Engagement** governs undertakings with a counterparty; **Knowledge** governs approved reusable artefacts. This document is the second of those.

It is written to be derived from, not redefined. Where implementation, interface or future engineering needs to know what binds us to a client or a vendor, and when, this is the authority.

---

# Article I · The constitutional question

> **What has been undertaken between the institution and another party, on what terms, and has it been discharged?**

Everything in this constitution answers that question. Nothing else belongs here.

Three words carry the whole of it. **Undertaken** — not intended, not planned, not recorded, but *owed*. **Another party** — someone outside the institution, whose acts we cannot perform. **Discharged** — undertakings end; they are not merely forgotten.

---

# Article II · Scope

## Inside

Undertakings with a counterparty and their discharge: what we offer, what they elect, what binds, what varies, what is owed in money, and what settles it.

## Outside, and why

| Jurisdiction | Governs | Why it is not Engagement |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlas** | assertions about our own subjects — work, evidence, states, ownership, and **experience** | no counterparty; nothing bilateral; nobody outside can change it |
| **Knowledge** | doctrine, Compositions, promotion, instantiation | artefacts, not undertakings; approved, never accepted; bind nobody |
| **Product** | why the product exists, what each workspace is for | philosophy states purpose; it cannot state what makes an undertaking binding |
| **Interaction** | how an operator meets any of this | grammar, not authority |
| **Dependency** | how a promise becomes work | causality, not jurisdiction |

## Explicitly not Engagement, though frequently mistaken for it

- **Inquiry.** Someone expressing interest undertakes nothing and obliges nobody. It is an assertion about our own subject — **Atlas**. The Sales stage named *Inquiry* is a workflow position, and the Dependency Map has already ruled that workflow is not structure.
- **Cost.** What we consumed is an assertion about our own operation — **Atlas**. Engagement consumes it for comparison against an agreed price and authors none of it.
- **The client's history, preferences and payment behaviour.** Observations about a subject — **Atlas** as experience, held in the Library, governed by neither.
- **Capacity.** What we are able to do is ours alone — **Atlas**. Only the *undertaking* to reserve it is Engagement.

# Article III · The counterparty

The counterparty is Engagement's defining distinction. Everything peculiar to this constitution follows from it.

**E‑III.1 · Definition.** A counterparty is a party outside the institution with whom undertakings are exchanged. A client, from whom consideration flows to us; a vendor, to whom it flows from us. The direction differs; the constitutional nature does not.

**E‑III.2 · The counterparty is the only actor outside the institution who may change our constitutional state.** No other constitution admits such an actor. Atlas's every fact is authored within the institution; Knowledge's every artefact is approved within it. Only here does an act performed by someone we do not employ, cannot instruct and may not simulate alter what is true.

**E‑III.3 · Their acts.** Acceptance, declination, election of an option, payment, and agreement to rescind or vary belong to them. **We may record that they occurred. We may never perform them.**

**E‑III.4 · Our acts.** Granting an option, issuing, revising, withdrawing, raising an obligation and applying value belong to us and require no consent.

**E‑III.5 · Bilateral acts.** Binding and unbinding are bilateral: an offer binds only when they accept, and a binding is undone only by their agreement or by a right they granted us in a term.

**E‑III.6 · The counterparty is a role, not an object.** The party exists independently of us. Engagement governs the *relation* — that this party stands on the other side of this undertaking — and never the party.

# Article IV · Canonical objects

Sixteen candidates were tested. **Seven survive.** The reductions are recorded because a constitution should show why it is small.

## The surviving objects

**E‑IV.1 · Offer** — the instrument of undertaking. A set of terms the institution is willing to be bound by, addressed to a counterparty. It is the only object capable of binding, and it is where every undertaking begins.

**E‑IV.2 · Term** — an atom of an Offer. What is undertaken, expressed so that it may be varied, elected between, and — once bound — become a specification.

**E‑IV.3 · Option** — a right of election granted to a counterparty, exercisable within a window, whose exercise determines a term or binds capacity. A *hold* is an option whose subject is capacity; a menu choice is an option whose subject is content. One species.

**E‑IV.4 · Acceptance** — the evidenced fact of a counterparty binding an Offer at a stated version.

**E‑IV.5 · Rescission** — the evidenced fact of an acceptance being undone.

**E‑IV.6 · Account Obligation** — money owed between the institution and a counterparty, in a stated direction, arising from a bound term or from a counterparty's assertion.

**E‑IV.7 · Payment** — the evidenced fact of value applied against an Account Obligation.

## The reductions

| Candidate | Disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Inquiry** | **eliminated** | undertakes nothing; an Atlas assertion |
| **Hold** | **merged into Option** | a right of election over capacity, with an expiry, binding us if exercised — structurally identical to a term-option |
| **Proposal** | **merged into Offer** | the drafted state of an Offer; *Proposal* is the product's word for it |
| **Promise** | **merged into Offer** | the **bound** state of an Offer. Where ratified documents say *the promise*, they mean an accepted Offer |
| **Commitment** | **eliminated** | not an object but the bound state |
| **Amendment** | **merged into Offer** | an Offer whose subject is a variation of a bound Offer. It is drafted, priced, issued and accepted exactly as any other. **The object is eliminated; the relation survives** |
| **Agreed price** | **merged into Term** | a term the counterparty agreed to. It may be computed from other terms; that does not make it a different species |
| **Receivable / Payable** | **merged into Account Obligation** | one species, two directions. The direction is constitutionally significant only for authorship (E‑XI.10) |
| **Credit** | **eliminated** | an Account Obligation in the counterparty's favour, arising from correction or concession, applied by set-off. No separate object is required |
| **Discharge of account** | **eliminated** | a derived state, not an object |
| **Account** | **eliminated** | the aggregate of Account Obligations with a counterparty. Storing it would be a rollup that stores, which PC‑9.13 forbids |
| **Counterparty** | **eliminated as object** | a role (Article III). The party exists without us |

**Sixteen to seven.** Every elimination is a merge or a relocation; nothing was discarded.

# Article V · Identity, state, version and supersession

**E‑V.1 · What possesses identity.** The **Offer** and the **Account Obligation**. These are the only objects that persist through change, and each is identified independently of its content.

**E‑V.2 · What versions.** The **Offer**. Its terms are revised repeatedly before it binds and, through varying offers, after. Every version survives; none is destroyed.

**E‑V.3 · What changes state.** The **Offer**, through: *drafted · issued · bound · declined · withdrawn · lapsed · superseded*. The **Option**, through: *granted · exercised · lapsed · withdrawn*. The **Account Obligation**, through: *raised · part-discharged · discharged · superseded*.

**E‑V.4 · What supersedes.** A revision supersedes a prior version of the same Offer. A varying offer, once bound, supersedes specific terms of the Offer it varies. A corrected Account Obligation supersedes its predecessor.

**E‑V.5 · What never changes identity.** An Offer's identity survives every revision, its acceptance, and every subsequent variation. **The thing the counterparty accepted and the thing that binds a year later are the same Offer.** An Account Obligation's identity survives part-payment, correction and set-off.

**E‑V.6 · What has no identity.** Terms, Acceptances, Rescissions and Payments have no independent identity. They exist only in relation to an object that has one. A term is a term *of* an Offer; a payment is applied *to* an obligation.

# Article VI · Ceremonies

A ceremony is an act with constitutional consequence. Ten survive derivation.

### 1 · Grant Option
**Preconditions** — a counterparty; a subject (capacity or a term); an expiry.
**Consequence** — the institution is bound not to defeat the election before expiry.
**Facts** — the option, its subject, its window, its grantee.
**Authority changed** — the counterparty acquires a right they did not have.

### 2 · Withdraw Option
**Preconditions** — an unexercised option; a right to withdraw, whether reserved or by agreement.
**Consequence** — the election is extinguished.
**Authority** — returns to the institution.
*Expiry is not this ceremony. Lapse is derived from the window and requires no act.*

### 3 · Issue Offer
**Preconditions** — a drafted Offer; a counterparty; a version.
**Consequence** — **the Offer becomes capable of binding.** This is the moment terms acquire constitutional weight; before it, they are drafting.
**Facts** — the issuance, its version, its recipient, its moment.
**Authority** — the counterparty acquires the power to bind us.

*A varying offer is issued by this ceremony and no other. There is no separate act of amendment.*

### 4 · Revise Offer
**Preconditions** — an Offer not yet bound.
**Consequence** — a new version supersedes the prior; any acceptance would be of the new version only.
**Authority** — unchanged; the counterparty's power now attaches to different terms.

### 5 · Withdraw Offer
**Preconditions** — an issued, unaccepted Offer.
**Consequence** — it can no longer bind.
**Authority** — the counterparty's power is extinguished.

### 6 · Accept — *bilateral*
**Preconditions** — an issued Offer, at a stated version, not withdrawn or lapsed; every option material to it elected or determinable.
**Consequence** — **the Offer binds.** Its terms become the undertaking; its price terms become determinable as Account Obligations; the subject becomes eligible for release.
**Facts** — the acceptance, the version accepted, the moment, the evidence of the counterparty's act.
**Authority** — the largest transfer in this constitution: from *we are willing* to *we are bound*.

### 7 · Decline — *counterparty*
**Preconditions** — an issued Offer.
**Consequence** — that Offer can no longer bind. Another may be issued.

### 8 · Rescind — *bilateral, or by reserved right*
**Preconditions** — a bound Offer; agreement, or a term reserving the right.
**Consequence** — the binding is undone. **The acceptance is not erased; a rescission is recorded against it.** Work already released is unaffected — see Article VIII.

### 9 · Raise Account Obligation
**Preconditions** — a bound term giving rise to money owed, or a counterparty's assertion that we owe.
**Consequence** — money becomes owed in a stated direction, at a stated time.
**Authority** — asymmetric. Obligations owed *to* us we assert. Obligations owed *by* us we recognise, because the counterparty asserted them.

### 10 · Apply
**Preconditions** — value — a payment received or made, or an obligation in the opposing direction — and an obligation to apply it against.
**Consequence** — the obligation is discharged in whole or in part.
**Facts** — the application, its amount, its moment.

## Ceremonies that did not survive derivation

**Amend** — it is *Issue Offer* (varying) followed by *Accept*. Amendments feel like proposals because they are proposals.

**Close Account** — discharge is derived from the obligations themselves. An act of closing would assert a state rather than derive it, and would create the stored rollup PC‑9.13 forbids.

**Lapse** — derived from a window. Time is not an actor.

# Article VII · Authority

| Object | Authored by | Observed by | May never modify |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Offer, Term** | the authoring environment for the promise, on behalf of Sales before binding and Event Management after | every workspace | **every operational workspace.** No station may alter a term |
| **Option** | Sales (capacity), the authoring environment (content) | every workspace | operations; Money |
| **Acceptance, Rescission** | recorded by Sales from the counterparty's act | every workspace | anyone. These record another party's act and may never be authored on their behalf |
| **Account Obligation** | Money | Owner, Event Management | every operational workspace. Money may never alter an operational fact to make an account agree |
| **Payment** | Money, recording the counterparty's act | Owner | anyone, on the counterparty's behalf |

**E‑VII.1 · Where authority begins.** At the first act creating a right or an obligation with a counterparty — ordinarily the grant of an option or the issuance of an offer.

**E‑VII.2 · Where authority ends.** When every Account Obligation is discharged and no Offer remains capable of binding. Engagement then has nothing further to say, though the Event File may remain open on operational grounds.

# Article VIII · Relationship to Atlas — the handoff

```
Acceptance   →   Release   →   Operational work
(Engagement)     (the handoff)   (Atlas)
```

**E‑VIII.1 · Why these are separate constitutional acts.**

**Acceptance binds us to an outcome. Release commits us to a method.** We may owe a client a plated dinner for two hundred without yet having decided how it will be produced, by whom, or in what order. Those are different undertakings to different audiences, and collapsing them would make method owed to the client and outcome owed to ourselves.

Four properties distinguish them, and any one alone is sufficient:

| | Acceptance | Release |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | **bilateral** — theirs | **unilateral** — ours |
| Creates | an undertaking *to them* | obligations *within us* |
| Object | terms | work obligations |
| Reversibility | rescindable by agreement | not reversible; evidence is appended |

**E‑VIII.2 · Release requires acceptance; acceptance does not compel release.** Nothing may be released against an Offer that does not bind. But a bound Offer may sit unreleased indefinitely, and the constitution takes no view on when release should occur — that is operational judgment.

**E‑VIII.3 · Rescission after release does not unmake work.** Work released is Atlas's, and Atlas is append-only. A rescission ends the undertaking; it does not undo what was done. What follows is an Account Obligation for work performed, not a deletion.

**E‑VIII.4 · Atlas never reads a term as an instruction.** It derives obligations from bound terms. The derivation is the Dependency Map's subject; the terms remain Engagement's and are never copied into Atlas as though they were its own.

# Article IX · Relationship to Knowledge

**E‑IX.1 · Instantiation** brings doctrine into an Offer as terms. **What is instantiated is copied and frozen at that moment** (PC‑5.11). From then on it is a term, governed here, and the Library has no further authority over it. A later change to doctrine never reaches a bound Offer.

**E‑IX.2 · Promotion** is entirely Knowledge's. Engagement takes no part, grants no consent and receives no notice. A promotion changes what may be instantiated tomorrow and nothing that was undertaken yesterday.

**E‑IX.3 · Doctrine and Composition remain outside Engagement** because they have no counterparty. A recipe binds nobody. A Composition is approved, not accepted — approval is internal governance, acceptance is bilateral consent, and the two are not the same act.

**E‑IX.4 · Experience is neither.** Observations about venues, suppliers and clients are Atlas assertions, held by the Library, governed by Atlas. Engagement may be informed by them and is never bound by them.

---

# Article X · Relationship to the Operational Constitution

**E‑X.1 · Engagement produces; operations consume.** A bound Offer's terms are the specification. Operational workspaces read them and derive work.

**E‑X.2 · No duplicate truth, because the terms are never copied.** Operations derive obligations *from* terms; they do not hold their own version of them. There is exactly one statement of what was undertaken, and it lives here.

**E‑X.3 · Divergence is recorded, never absorbed.** When reality departs from a term — a substitution, an overrun, a count that moved — the term does not change. Either a varying offer is issued and accepted, or an operational fact records what actually occurred. **What was undertaken and what happened are both true, and neither overwrites the other.**

This is the single most important protection in this article. A system that let operations quietly amend terms would leave nobody able to say what was promised.

**E‑X.4 · The frozen Operational Constitution is unaffected by this document.** Engagement adds no operational article, alters no workspace's ownership, and introduces no operational ceremony.

---

# Article XI · The laws

**E‑XI.1** Engagement governs undertakings with a counterparty and nothing else.

**E‑XI.2** Every undertaking has a counterparty. An undertaking without one is an Atlas obligation and does not belong here.

**E‑XI.3** Nothing binds without an evidenced act of the counterparty.

**E‑XI.4** The institution may never author an act belonging to the counterparty. Acceptance, declination, election and payment may be *recorded*; they may never be *asserted* on their behalf.

**E‑XI.5** An Offer binds only in the version accepted.

**E‑XI.6** A term is never edited. It is superseded by a further accepted Offer, or it stands.

**E‑XI.7** Divergence between an undertaking and reality is recorded, never absorbed.

**E‑XI.8** Acceptance binds outcome; release commits method. They are distinct acts with distinct authorities and may not be merged.

**E‑XI.9** Price is a term. It is agreed with everything else and is not owned separately.

**E‑XI.10** Money owed is directional, and authorship follows direction: what is owed to us we assert; what is owed by us we recognise.

**E‑XI.11** The account is derived and never stored. Discharge is a consequence of obligations, not a state anyone may set.

**E‑XI.12** Expiry is derived. Time performs no ceremony.

**E‑XI.13** Doctrine instantiated into an Offer is frozen at instantiation and thereafter governed here.

**E‑XI.14** Engagement authors no operational fact; operations author no term.

**E‑XI.15** Nothing is deleted. Rescissions, corrections and set-offs are appended; what was true remains answerable.

**E‑XI.16** Every Engagement truth is answerable as of a moment. What was undertaken on a date is knowable on that date's terms.

# Reduction audit

*Each object and ceremony attacked in turn.*

**Offer.** Eliminate it? Nothing else can be accepted, and acceptance is the constitution's central act. **Survives.**

**Term.** Could an Offer be atomic? Then amendments could not target parts of it, options could not elect between parts, and the promise-as-specification property could not operate element by element. **Survives.**

**Option.** Is it merely an unaccepted offer? No — a hold is granted before any offer exists, and electing a term has consequence before acceptance. Neither reduces to the other. **Survives.**

**Acceptance.** The defining act. **Survives.**

**Rescission.** Could it be acceptance superseded? Under append-only nothing is erased, so undoing must be recorded as its own fact. **Survives.**

**Account Obligation.** Could money be a term? A term states *the price is $27,500*; an obligation states *$8,250 is due on 1 June*. Terms are agreed once; obligations are raised repeatedly, in instalments, against amendments and after performance. **Survives.**

**Payment.** Nothing else discharges. **Survives.**

**Counterparty.** Eliminate it and Engagement loses its only distinction from Atlas, and the reduction that created this constitution collapses. **Cannot be eliminated.**

**Agreed price.** Could it live only in Money? Then the counterparty would be bound to a price that was not in what they accepted. **Cannot be eliminated — but it is a Term, not an object.**

**Amendments.** Attacked hardest, as instructed. A varying offer is drafted, priced, issued, accepted or declined — every property of an Offer, and no property an Offer lacks. **The object is eliminated. The relation survives**, and the ceremony *Amend* dissolves into *Issue* and *Accept*.

**Reduce Engagement into Atlas?** Atlas has no counterparty, no bilateral consent, no election, no act performed by someone outside the institution. Granting it these would change what Atlas is. **Fails.**

**Reduce Engagement into Knowledge?** Knowledge has no counterparty and holds artefacts rather than undertakings; Engagement holds no artefacts. **Fails.**

**Merge Grant Option into Issue Offer?** A hold precedes any offer. **Fails.**

**Merge Raise Account Obligation into Accept?** Obligations are raised in instalments, on schedules, after performance, and against amendments — long after and repeatedly. **Fails.**

**Merge Apply into Raise?** One creates what is owed; the other extinguishes it. **Fails.**

# Final verdict

## Is the Engagement Constitution constitutionally complete?

**Yes.**

Its question is single. Its scope is bounded by three principled distinctions — counterparty, artefact, assertion — and overlaps none of the other five documents. Its objects reduce from sixteen candidates to seven, each surviving direct attack. Its ceremonies reduce to ten, with two of the proposed acts dissolving into others. Its authority is assigned without duplication. Its handoffs to Atlas and Knowledge are stated as transfers of authority rather than as flows of data. It contradicts no ratified ruling and adds no article to the frozen Operational Constitution.

## One open edge, recorded rather than resolved

**Undertakings above the Event File.** PC‑4.8 places a client account above the file, holding standing terms — an undertaking with a counterparty that attaches to a relationship rather than to one engagement. Within the current freeze, every undertaking attaches to an Event File, and this constitution is complete on that basis.

This is **not** an unresolved constitutional question for Engagement. Its objects, ceremonies and laws apply unchanged whatever the subject turns out to be; only the subject's location awaits the Client Account being constitutionally placed. It is recorded here so that the placement, when it happens, is recognised as locating a subject rather than amending this document.

## What remains, and what kind of work it is

**Implementation** — realising the seven objects, the ten ceremonies and the sixteen laws; deriving Account Obligations from bound price terms; recording acceptance, rescission and payment as evidenced facts; the handoff from acceptance to release.

**Product refinement** — how an amendment's consequence is shown before it is made; how an option's expiry is surfaced; how divergence between term and reality is presented without implying either is wrong.

**Operator validation** — whether *hold* and *option* being one object survives contact with people who experience them as different things; whether recording divergence rather than absorbing it matches how operators actually work; whether the acceptance/release separation is legible or merely correct.

**No further constitutional work is required for Engagement.** One jurisdiction remains unwritten — Knowledge — and with it the constitutional architecture of EventCore is complete.
