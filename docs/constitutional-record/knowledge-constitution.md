# The Knowledge Constitution

*Constitutional law. It governs the institution's approved reusable knowledge.*

---

## Preamble

The Constitutional Reduction established three jurisdictions over truth: **Atlas** governs assertions about our own subjects; **Engagement** governs undertakings with a counterparty; **Knowledge** governs approved reusable artefacts. This is the third and last.

It is the smallest of the three, and deliberately so. Its entire content concerns a single distinction that neither of the others can make: the difference between something the institution *observed* and something the institution *holds as how it does things*.

---

# Article I · The constitutional question

> **What does the institution hold as approved and reusable, who may approve it, and what happens when it is used?**

Three words carry it. **Approved** — not merely recorded, not merely written down, but adopted by an authority as the institution's own. **Reusable** — intended for occasions that have not happened yet. **Used** — because the moment of use is where authority transfers, and that is the most consequential act in this constitution.

---

# Article II · Scope

## Inside

What the institution holds as its own way of doing things: how a dish is made, how a room is set, how a station is composed, what a package contains, what standards apply. Their authorship, approval, versioning, retirement, and what occurs when they are put to use.

## Outside, and why

| Jurisdiction | Governs | Why it is not Knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlas** | assertions about subjects — work, evidence, states, and **experience** | an observation is a claim about something; doctrine is not a claim at all |
| **Engagement** | undertakings with a counterparty | doctrine binds nobody; approval is not acceptance |
| **Product** | why knowledge exists and what the Library is for | purpose, not authority |
| **Interaction** | how doctrine is browsed and composed | grammar, not authority |
| **Dependency** | that doctrine translates a promise into work | causality, not jurisdiction |

## Explicitly not Knowledge, though frequently held alongside it

- **Experience.** A venue's dock closes at four. A supplier is unreliable. A client's mother is coeliac. These are observations about subjects, made by an observer at a moment, superseded by later observation — **Atlas**. The Library *holds* them; Atlas *governs* them. Custody and authority are different things.
- **Operational history.** What was actually done at an event is a record of that occasion — **Atlas**. It may become the raw material for knowledge, but it is not knowledge.
- **The promise.** Once instantiated, doctrine becomes a term of an Offer — **Engagement**. Article VIII governs the transfer.
- **The taxonomy of doctrine.** Recipe, standard, pattern, template, blueprint — these are *kinds*, and kinds are domain vocabulary. A constitution names species, not taxonomy.

# Article III · Canonical objects

Fourteen candidates were tested. **Three survive.**

## The surviving objects

**K‑III.1 · Doctrine** — knowledge the institution has approved as its own way of doing things, held for reuse on occasions that have not yet occurred. It asserts nothing about the world; it declares how the institution intends to act.

**K‑III.2 · Composition** — doctrine that may be instantiated into an Offer as a term. A Sushi Station, a package, a layout: something a counterparty can be offered. It is a species of Doctrine, distinguished by exactly one constitutional property, and that property is jurisdictional — **it may cross into Engagement; other doctrine may not.**

**K‑III.3 · Candidate** — knowledge extracted from a completed engagement, awaiting promotion. **A candidate is not doctrine and is not reusable.** It exists solely to be promoted or to remain unpromoted.

## The reductions

| Candidate | Disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Recipe · Standard · Pattern · Template · Blueprint** | **merged into Doctrine** | five kinds, one species. Identical authorship, approval, versioning, retirement and use. A constitution that enumerated kinds would need amending every time the business named a new one |
| **Library Entry** | **eliminated** | doctrine as displayed. Interaction, not authority |
| **Version** | **eliminated as object** | a state of Doctrine. Doctrine possesses identity; versions are how it changes |
| **Approval** | **eliminated as object** | a ceremony, producing a fact |
| **Promotion** | **eliminated as object** | a ceremony |
| **Instantiation** | **eliminated as object** | a ceremony — and the most important one |
| **Retirement** | **eliminated as object** | a ceremony and a resulting state |
| **Supersession** | **eliminated as object** | a relation, arising as a consequence of approval |

**Fourteen to three.** The reduction is severe because most candidates were either kinds of one thing or acts upon it.

## Why Composition survives, having been attacked

Composition shares every lifecycle property with other doctrine: it is authored, approved, versioned, retired, and may be promoted from a candidate. On those grounds it should merge.

It survives on one ground only, and the ground is jurisdictional. **A Composition may be instantiated into an Offer; other doctrine may not.** A client is offered a Sushi Station, not a sushi recipe. The recipe reaches the event as *method*; the station reaches it as *term*.

That distinction determines which artefacts may cross a constitutional boundary, and a constitution must name what crosses its own borders. Were Composition merely a property of doctrine, the boundary would be a flag; because the boundary transfers authority to another constitution, it is a species.

**It is neither historical experience nor operational work.** Not experience, because it asserts nothing about any occasion and has no observer. Not operational work, because nothing is owed and nobody performs it. It sits in the only remaining category: a thing the institution holds.

# Article IV · Doctrine

**K‑IV.1 · Definition.** Doctrine is approved, reusable, occasion-independent knowledge of how the institution acts.

**K‑IV.2 · Doctrine is normative, not factual.** It is not true or false. It is authoritative or not, current or superseded, in force or retired. **Nothing evidences a recipe.**

**K‑IV.3 · Doctrine has no subject.** An observation is *about* something — a venue, a supplier, an event. Doctrine is about nothing; it is a thing.

**K‑IV.4 · Doctrine is occasion-independent.** The moment doctrine records why a particular event went a particular way, it has ceased to be doctrine and become history.

## Doctrine distinguished

| | Doctrine | Experience | Operational history | Promise | Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Is it a claim?** | no | **yes**, about a subject | **yes**, about an occasion | no — an undertaking | no |
| **Can it be wrong?** | no — only outdated | **yes** | **yes** | no — only unmet | — |
| **Occasion-bound?** | **never** | to an observation | **entirely** | to one engagement | — |
| **Governed by** | Knowledge | Atlas | Atlas | Engagement | none |

**K‑IV.5** The most important row is *can it be wrong*. An observation may be mistaken and later corrected. Doctrine cannot be mistaken — it can be poor, obsolete or replaced, but never false. **Truth and authority are different failure modes, and no single constitution can govern both.** This is the whole reason Knowledge is separate from Atlas.

---

# Article V · Identity, version, supersession and immutability

**K‑V.1 · What possesses identity.** **Doctrine** (including Compositions) and **Candidates**.

**K‑V.2 · What versions.** Doctrine. Every approved version survives; none is destroyed.

**K‑V.3 · What supersedes.** The approval of a new version supersedes its predecessor for future use. Supersession is a *consequence* of approval, never a separate act.

**K‑V.4 · What never changes identity.** Doctrine's identity survives every version, every supersession and its retirement. **The Sushi Station is the same Sushi Station at version one and version nine.** Identity is what makes it possible to ask *what is our Sushi Station now* and *which version did we promise* — two questions that would be unanswerable if each revision created a new thing.

**K‑V.5 · What is immutable once approved.** **An approved version.** It is never edited. To change doctrine is to author a new version and approve it. Anything else would silently alter what was instantiated into offers already sent.

**K‑V.6 · What has no identity.** Approvals, promotions, retirements and instantiations are facts about objects that have identity. They have none of their own.

# Article VI · Ceremonies

Ten candidates were derived. **Six survive.**

### 1 · Author
**Preconditions** — none.
**Consequence** — an unapproved version exists. **It is not doctrine and may not be used.**
**Facts** — the version, its author, its moment.
**Authority** — none transfers. Authoring creates nothing usable.
*Revision is this ceremony. Authoring a first version and a ninth are the same act.*

### 2 · Approve
**Preconditions** — an unapproved version; an approving authority.
**Consequence** — **the version becomes doctrine.** It may be instantiated and consulted; it supersedes its predecessor for future use; instantiations already made are unaffected.
**Facts** — the approval, its authority, its moment, the version approved.
**Authority** — the institution adopts the version as its own. This is the ceremony that makes something reusable, and nothing else does.

### 3 · Extract
**Preconditions** — a completed engagement.
**Consequence** — a **Candidate** exists. It is not doctrine, is not reusable, and confers nothing.
**Facts** — the candidate, its source engagement, its extractor, its moment.
**Authority** — none. **Extraction observes; it decides nothing.**

### 4 · Promote
**Preconditions** — a Candidate; an approving authority; and one requirement no other ceremony carries — **the candidate must be separated from its occasion.**
**Consequence** — doctrine exists, derived from what happened but no longer a record of it.
**Facts** — the promotion, its authority, its moment, the candidate promoted.
**Authority** — the institution adopts as its intention something that began as an incident.

### 5 · Retire
**Preconditions** — doctrine in force.
**Consequence** — it may no longer be instantiated or consulted for new work. **Existing instantiations are wholly unaffected.**
**Facts** — the retirement, its authority, its moment.
*Distinct from supersession: supersession replaces, retirement withdraws without replacing.*

### 6 · Instantiate
**Preconditions** — an approved, unretired Composition; an Offer under authorship.
**Consequence** — **authority transfers.** Article VIII governs it in full.
**Facts** — a term of the Offer, recording which doctrine at which version.
**Authority** — Knowledge's authority over the copy ends absolutely and immediately.

## Ceremonies that did not survive derivation

**Submit** — nothing changes constitutionally when a draft is offered for approval. It is workflow, and the Dependency Map has ruled workflow is not structure.

**Reject** — absence of approval is already sufficient. A rejected draft is simply a draft, and a candidate not promoted is simply a candidate.

**Publish** — approved doctrine is usable. If publication were a further condition, approval would not be approval.

**Supersede** — a consequence of approval, not an act.

**Withdraw** — a candidate that is never promoted never becomes knowledge. Discarding it is housekeeping.

# Article VII · Authority

| Object | Authored by | Approved by | Observed by | May never modify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Doctrine** | anyone the institution permits to author | **an approving authority** | every workspace, every constitution | **anyone, once approved** — a version is immutable |
| **Composition** | as doctrine | as doctrine | every workspace; the authoring environment for offers | as doctrine |
| **Candidate** | produced by extraction | promoted, or not | those who may promote | — |
| **An instantiated copy** | — | — | — | **Knowledge, absolutely** |

## Four acts that are not the same act

**K‑VII.1 · Authoring** creates. It confers nothing. An authored version is inert.

**K‑VII.2 · Approval** adopts. It is internal, unilateral, and it makes a thing usable. It creates **availability**, never obligation.

**K‑VII.3 · Promotion** adopts *something that was history*. It is approval carrying one additional constitutional burden — separation from occasion — and it exists because that burden exists.

**K‑VII.4 · Instantiation** uses. It transfers authority out of this constitution. It approves nothing and creates no doctrine.

Conflating any two of these produces a specific failure. Authoring taken as approval fills the Library with drafts. Approval taken as promotion lets incident become intention. Instantiation taken as approval lets a promise create doctrine. Promotion taken as saving preserves the past instead of learning from it.

## Who holds the approving authority

**K‑VII.5** This constitution establishes **that** approval requires an authority and **what** that authority must find. It does not name the holder, exactly as Atlas does not name who may claim a responsibility and Engagement does not name who may sign on the institution's behalf. Naming the holder is institutional policy operating within this law, not law itself.

---

# Article VIII · Instantiation

The most consequential article in this constitution.

**K‑VIII.1 · What occurs.** A copy of an approved version becomes a **term** of an Offer.

**K‑VIII.2 · What transfers.** **Authority.** The copy passes wholly into Engagement's jurisdiction. From that instant Knowledge cannot amend it, supersede it, retire it, or reach it in any way.

**K‑VIII.3 · What freezes.** The copy. It is fixed at the version instantiated, permanently. A doctrine revision approved the following day does not touch an offer sent yesterday.

**K‑VIII.4 · Why it must freeze.** Because otherwise a revision to doctrine would alter what a counterparty agreed to. A client who accepted a Sushi Station would find themselves holding a different one, changed by an internal decision they never saw. **That is the second truth the entire constitutional architecture exists to prevent**, and it would arrive by the quietest possible route.

**K‑VIII.5 · What remains with Knowledge.** The doctrine itself, its identity, its versions, its future. Instantiation consumes nothing. Doctrine may be instantiated a thousand times and is undiminished.

**K‑VIII.6 · Provenance.** The term records which doctrine at which version produced it. That record belongs to the term, and therefore to Engagement. Without it, no one could later ask what was promised and on what basis.

**K‑VIII.7 · The second path: consultation.** Doctrine also reaches operations without passing through an Offer. A service standard, a packing method, a labour ratio — these are consulted rather than instantiated.

**Consultation transfers no authority and freezes nothing.** Operations read the **current** doctrine.

**K‑VIII.8 · The asymmetry, and why it is exactly right.**

> **Instantiated doctrine is frozen. Consulted doctrine is live.**

This is not an inconsistency. It is the acceptance/release distinction expressed in artefacts. **Acceptance binds outcome; release commits method.** What the counterparty was promised is frozen, because it is owed to them. How we produce it is current, because it is ours to improve.

A client is owed a Sushi Station as offered. They are not owed the sushi-handling standard we held that week. If we improved it yesterday, today's event benefits — and nothing promised has changed.

---

# Article IX · Promotion

**K‑IX.1 · When historical experience becomes institutional knowledge.** Never automatically, never by accumulation, and never by the passage of time. Only by an authority's act.

**K‑IX.2 · What promotion requires.** That the candidate be **separated from its occasion**. Doctrine that records *why this event went this way* is history wearing doctrine's clothes.

**K‑IX.3 · What the authority must find.** That the candidate is generalisable beyond the engagement it came from; that its occasion has been stripped; and that it does not contradict doctrine already in force without superseding it.

**K‑IX.4 · Why promotion is not merely saving something.**

> **Saving preserves an occasion. Promotion asserts *this is how we do things*.**

A saved record says *this happened*. Promoted doctrine says *this is what we intend*. The first is a claim about the past and belongs to Atlas. The second is a norm about the future and belongs here. **They are not the same act, and the gap between them is where a catering business either learns or merely accumulates.**

**K‑IX.5 · Why the gate must exist.** Without it, extraction would produce doctrine directly and the Library would fill with everything that ever happened — including the substitutions made when a supplier failed, the shortcuts taken when a crew was short, and the improvisations that worked once. A company that reuses its incidents becomes a museum of its own workarounds.

**K‑IX.6 · What the loop is.** Operations produce facts (Atlas). Facts become candidates (Extract). Candidates become doctrine (Promote). Doctrine becomes terms (Instantiate) and method (Consult). **This is the institution's learning, constitutionally specified end to end** — and promotion is the only point in it where a person decides.

# Article X · Relationship to the other jurisdictions

## To Atlas

**K‑X.1** An observation is a **claim about a subject**, made by an observer at a moment, superseded by later observation, and answerable as of any date. Doctrine is a **thing the institution holds**, with versions and an approval, about nothing.

**K‑X.2** They must remain separate because **they fail differently.** An observation can be wrong. Doctrine cannot — it can only be poor, outdated or retired. A constitution governing both would need one discipline for correcting error and another for changing intention, and would be two constitutions in one document.

**K‑X.3** The Library holds both. Custody is not authority.

## To Engagement

**K‑X.4 · How doctrine becomes terms.** By instantiation, and only by instantiation. Article VIII governs it.

**K‑X.5 · Why approval is fundamentally different from acceptance.**

| | Approval | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| Who acts | the institution | **the counterparty** |
| Nature | unilateral, internal | bilateral, external |
| Creates | **availability** | **obligation** |
| Binds | nobody | us |

**An approved Composition binds no one.** It becomes available to be offered. Only acceptance binds, and only Engagement can host that act.

**K‑X.6 · Why instantiated doctrine leaves Knowledge.** Because it has become part of an undertaking with a counterparty, and Knowledge has no counterparty and no means of accounting for one. Retaining authority would let an internal approval alter an external undertaking.

## To the Operational Constitution

**K‑X.7** Operations consume doctrine two ways: as **terms** (frozen, via the Offer) and as **method** (live, by consultation). Neither confers authority over doctrine.

**K‑X.8** An operator who works differently from doctrine does not amend it. They produce an operational fact recording what was done — Atlas. If that ought to become doctrine, it travels Extract → Promote, like everything else. **Practice never silently becomes policy.**

## To the Product Constitution

**K‑X.9** Product says **why** knowledge exists: institutional memory must outlive the people who hold it, and a company's real asset is what it has learned. Knowledge says **what institutional knowledge constitutionally is**: an approved, versioned, occasion-independent artefact, immutable once approved, whose use transfers authority.

Purpose cannot say what makes something authoritative, who may approve it, or what happens when it is used. That is why this constitution exists rather than being a chapter of Product.

---

# Article XI · The laws

**K‑XI.1** Knowledge governs what the institution holds as approved and reusable, and nothing else.

**K‑XI.2** Doctrine asserts nothing. It cannot be true or false — only in force, superseded or retired.

**K‑XI.3** Nothing is doctrine until approved. Authored is not approved; extracted is not approved; used is not approved.

**K‑XI.4** An approved version is immutable. Doctrine changes by authoring and approving a new version, never by editing an existing one.

**K‑XI.5** Doctrine's identity survives every version, supersession and retirement.

**K‑XI.6** Supersession is a consequence of approval. Retirement withdraws without replacing. Neither reaches an existing instantiation.

**K‑XI.7** Extraction observes; promotion decides. A candidate is not knowledge.

**K‑XI.8** Promotion requires separation from occasion. Doctrine that carries the history of a particular engagement is not doctrine.

**K‑XI.9** Instantiation transfers authority absolutely. Knowledge retains none over the copy.

**K‑XI.10** Instantiated doctrine is frozen at its version. Consulted doctrine is current. Outcome is owed; method is ours.

**K‑XI.11** Instantiation consumes nothing. Doctrine is undiminished by any number of uses.

**K‑XI.12** Approval creates availability, never obligation. Only acceptance binds.

**K‑XI.13** Practice never silently becomes policy. Divergence is recorded as an operational fact and becomes doctrine only through promotion.

**K‑XI.14** Nothing is deleted. Superseded and retired versions remain answerable.

**K‑XI.15** Every Knowledge truth is answerable as of a moment. What was in force on a date is knowable on that date's terms.

# Reduction audit

**Composition.** Eliminate into Doctrine? It shares every lifecycle property. It survives on one ground: **it may cross into Engagement as a term, and other doctrine may not.** A constitution must name what crosses its borders. **Survives.**

**Doctrine.** The genus. Eliminate it and nothing remains to govern. **Survives.**

**Candidate.** Eliminate it and extraction produces doctrine directly — the promotion gate disappears, and incident becomes intention automatically. **Survives.**

**Promotion.** Is it merely Approve applied to a candidate? It carries one precondition approval does not: separation from occasion. That precondition is constitutional, because without it PC‑6's prohibition on copying institutional history is unenforceable. **Survives.**

**Instantiation.** Is it merely reading? No — it transfers authority to another constitution and freezes a copy permanently. Nothing else in the architecture performs a transfer of jurisdiction. **Survives, and is the most consequential ceremony here.**

**Approval.** Eliminate it and everything authored is doctrine. The Library fills with drafts, and *reusable* loses its meaning. **Survives.**

**Versioning.** Could doctrine be immutable, each change producing a new thing? Then identity fragments: one could not ask *what is our Sushi Station now*, nor *which version did we promise*. Both questions are load-bearing — the second is how a promise stays answerable. **Survives.**

**Reduce Knowledge into Atlas?** Atlas governs claims about subjects, evidenced and derived. Doctrine has no subject, no observer, no evidence, and cannot be wrong. Extending Atlas to artefacts would make it answer two unrelated questions. **Fails.**

**Reduce Knowledge into Engagement?** Engagement's defining distinction is the counterparty. Doctrine has none, binds nobody, and is approved rather than accepted. **Fails.**

**Reduce Knowledge into Product?** Product is philosophy. It can say why institutional memory matters; it cannot say what makes an artefact authoritative, who may approve it, or what happens to authority when it is used. **Fails.**

# Final verdict

## Is the Knowledge Constitution constitutionally complete?

**Yes.**

Its question is single. Its scope is bounded by two principled distinctions — assertion against artefact, and counterparty against none — and overlaps no other document. Its objects reduce from fourteen candidates to three, each surviving direct attack. Its ceremonies reduce from ten to six. Its four acts — authoring, approval, promotion, instantiation — are held distinct, with the specific failure of conflating each pair stated. Its central article, instantiation, resolves the freeze/live asymmetry as an expression of the already-ratified acceptance/release distinction rather than as an exception to anything.

**The question left open by the Constitutional Reduction is resolved rather than deferred.** *Who may promote* is institutional policy operating within K‑VII.5, not constitutional law — exactly as Atlas does not name who may claim a responsibility and Engagement does not name who signs. What is constitutional is that promotion requires an authority (K‑IX.1) and what that authority must find (K‑IX.3).

## What remains, and what kind of work it is

**Implementation** — the three objects; the six ceremonies; version identity and immutability; the freeze at instantiation and provenance on the term; extraction from completed engagements; the retirement rule that spares existing instantiations.

**Product refinement** — how the Library presents doctrine beside experience without implying one authority; how a promotion authority reviews a candidate; how consultation surfaces current doctrine at the moment of work; what ships as seeded doctrine on the first day.

**Operator validation** — whether the debrief that produces candidates actually happens; whether separation from occasion is a judgment operators can make; whether the Library survives being empty; whether frozen-outcome and live-method reads as obviously right at the bench or merely as correct on paper.

# The constitutional architecture of EventCore is complete

Every truth the system can hold now has exactly one governing authority.

| Jurisdiction | Governs | Distinguished by |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlas** | assertions about our own subjects — work, evidence, states, ownership, experience | a claim, about a subject, that can be wrong |
| **Engagement** | undertakings with a counterparty | a party outside the institution whose acts we cannot perform |
| **Knowledge** | approved reusable artefacts | a thing we hold, that cannot be wrong, only outdated |

Three constitutions of truth, distinguished by three tests that are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive: **is there a counterparty?** — if yes, Engagement. **Is it a claim about a subject?** — if yes, Atlas. **Is it a thing we hold?** — if yes, Knowledge. No truth in a catering business escapes all three, and none satisfies two.

Around them stand four documents that govern something other than truth: **Product** (why the product exists), **Interaction** (how an operator meets it), **Dependency** (how one truth becomes another), and the **Operational Freeze** (what is settled and what is deliberately not).

Seven documents. No overlap. No duplicate authority. No unlocated capability.

**The three defects the audit found are each resolved or located:** Money now has a jurisdiction and a set of canonical objects; the doctrine/experience split is a constitutional line rather than a product preference; and every act that was previously described only philosophically — acceptance, release, approval, promotion, instantiation — is now an act with preconditions, consequences and an authority.

**Future work should proceed through implementation, product refinement, and operator validation, not through further constitutional expansion.** The architecture's completeness is not a claim that nothing will change; it is a claim that **change now has a place to happen.** A new capability is located by asking the three tests. A disagreement is resolved by citing an article. A proposed feature is judged by the Product Constitution and expressed through the Interaction Constitution. None of that requires a new constitution, and each new constitution written from here would necessarily overlap one that already exists.

The constitutional phase of EventCore is closed.
