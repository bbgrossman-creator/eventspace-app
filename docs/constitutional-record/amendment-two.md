# Amendment Two to the EventCore Constitutional Framework

**Adopted 5 August 2026. Supersedes Correction 2 of Amendment One. Correction 1 of Amendment One stands unaltered.**

## The defect

Amendment One's first test read:

> *Does a counterparty's act create, vary or discharge an undertaking?*

It routes acceptance, election, payment and agreed rescission correctly. It fails on every institutional act:

| Engagement ceremony | Actor | Amendment One routing |
|---|---|---|
| Grant Option | institution | **falls through → Atlas** ✗ |
| Withdraw Option | institution | **falls through → Atlas** ✗ |
| Issue Offer | institution | **falls through → Atlas** ✗ |
| Revise Offer | institution | **falls through → Atlas** ✗ |
| Withdraw Offer | institution | **falls through → Atlas** ✗ |
| Raise Account Obligation | institution | **falls through → Atlas** ✗ |
| Apply | institution | **falls through → Atlas** ✗ |
| Accept · Decline · Rescind | counterparty | Engagement ✓ |

Seven of ten ceremonies mis-routed. The correction was over-narrow: it fixed a false positive by introducing false negatives across most of the constitution it was meant to serve.

## The jurisdictional invariant

The error came from testing for an *actor* when the invariant concerns a *source of significance*. Engagement's invariant is:

> **Does this fact or act derive its constitutional significance from an undertaking between the institution and a counterparty?**

Not *is a counterparty present* — the Marriott may be a counterparty while a claim about its loading dock is not an Engagement truth. Not *did a counterparty act* — the institution issues offers unilaterally and they are unquestionably Engagement's.

The invariant governs. The tests below merely operationalise it.

## Correction 2 (restated) · The routing tests

### Superseded text — Amendment One, replacement for Article III.1

> **1 · Does a counterparty's act create, vary or discharge an undertaking?** → Engagement
> **2 · Otherwise, is it a claim about a subject?** → Atlas
> **3 · Otherwise, is it a thing we hold as approved and reusable?** → Knowledge

### Replacement text

> **Article III.1** — Truth is partitioned by three tests that are **ordered and jurisdictionally exclusive**. They are applied in sequence, and the first that answers governs. Each operationalises its constitution's invariant; where a test and its invariant appear to diverge, **the invariant governs and the test is defective**.
>
> > **1 · Is it an undertaking with a counterparty, a constitutional act upon such an undertaking, or a fact whose consequence is to create, vary, evidence or discharge one?** → **Engagement**
> >
> > **2 · Otherwise, is it an artefact the institution holds as approved and reusable, or a constitutional act upon such an artefact?** → **Knowledge**
> >
> > **3 · Otherwise, is it a claim about a subject?** → **Atlas**
>
> **Why the order is principled rather than convenient.** Many facts are describable under more than one test. A payment is a counterparty's act *and* an evidenced occurrence; an approval is a governance decision *and* a recordable fact. The order resolves them because **constitutional significance follows the source of consequence**. What matters about a payment is not that money moved but that an obligation was discharged by a party we cannot compel. What matters about an approval is not that a decision was recorded but that an artefact became reusable.
>
> **Why Atlas is last.** Atlas is the ground, and therefore the residual. Anything that is a claim about a subject and derives its significance neither from an undertaking nor from an artefact is Atlas's. Placing it earlier would let it absorb the acts of the other two, since every constitutional act is also describable as something that occurred.
>
> **This is a routing order, not a precedence order.** The three truth constitutions remain peers under Article IV, Level 1. Routing decides *which document answers*; precedence would decide *which document wins*, and among peers no such question arises.
>
> **On exhaustiveness.** No truth has been found that answers none of the three — a finding established by the attack in Article VIII.1, sustained until a counterexample is produced, not a claim of logical necessity. The burden of producing one rests on whoever asserts a new jurisdiction (VI.3).

## The symmetric defect, and why it must be corrected here

Amendment One's third test read *"is it a thing we hold as approved and reusable?"* — it catches artefacts but **not the constitutional acts upon them.** This is the identical defect, in the identical form, one test later.

Knowledge has six ceremonies: Author, Approve, Extract, Promote, Retire, Instantiate. Each produces a fact. Under the superseded ordering, *"X approved doctrine D version 4 on 3 March"* is a claim about a subject, reaches the Atlas test first, and routes to **Atlas** — though K‑VI.2 assigns approvals unambiguously to Knowledge.

Correcting only the test that was pointed at would have left the corpus with one repaired routing rule and one containing the same fault. The replacement text above therefore extends the artefact test to acts upon artefacts, **and this is what requires Knowledge to precede Atlas** — otherwise the residual absorbs the acts before the artefact test is reached.

## Procedural note

Adopted under Article VI.1, appended and dated with superseded text retained under VI.4.

Two amendments in two days, both to the same passage, both correcting over-claim rather than architecture. The first asserted a logical property the tests did not have. The second corrected it by narrowing so far that most of Engagement fell out — and the same fault, unnoticed, sat one test further down.

The lesson worth recording is not that the corpus was fragile. It is that **the routing tests are the most error-prone thing in it**, because they are a summary of three constitutions in three sentences, and a summary can be wrong while everything it summarises is right. Article III.1 now states the invariant above the tests, so that a future defect in the operationalisation is caught against the principle rather than propagated by it.

The corpus is ready for review.
