# Amendment One to the EventCore Constitutional Framework

**Adopted 5 August 2026. Two corrections. No jurisdiction, order of interpretation, or verdict is altered.**

---

## Correction 1 · The corpus contains eight documents

### Defect

The Framework counts "seven documents" in Article II and in the Final Verdict, while the Constitutional Index in Article IX enumerates **eight rows**, the first of which is the Framework itself. The document uses two concepts — *the corpus* and *the documents the corpus governs* — without ever distinguishing them.

This is a clerical inconsistency, not an architectural one. But an opening document that miscounts its own contents invites the reader to doubt everything after it.

### Superseded text

> **Article II, preamble** — "Seven documents. Three assert truth; one explains propagation; three direct construction."

> **Article VI.3** — "…requires demonstrating a jurisdiction none of the seven covers…"

> **Final Verdict** — "Seven documents. Three partition truth…"

### Replacement text

> **Article II, preamble** — **The corpus contains eight documents: this Framework and seven governed constitutional documents.** Of the seven, three assert truth, one explains propagation, and three direct construction. The Framework asserts no truth, governs no jurisdiction, and directs no construction; it governs the other seven.

> **Article VI.3** — "…requires demonstrating a jurisdiction none of the **seven governed documents** covers…"

> **Final Verdict** — "**Eight documents: this Framework and seven it governs.** Of the seven, three partition truth…"

### Consequential clarifications

**Article III.1** — "The remaining four documents govern something other than truth" is correct and unchanged; it refers to the four non-truth documents among the seven governed. It is clarified to read "**the remaining four governed documents**."

**Article IX** — the Index is correct as written and requires no change. Its eight rows are the corpus. Its first row, marked *(this)*, is the governing document; the seven below it are the governed. A single line is added beneath the table:

> *Eight rows: this Framework and the seven documents it governs.*

**Article VIII.1** — the finding that "the corpus itself is governed by this document, which is why the corpus is not an object of one of its own members" is unchanged and is the reason the Framework holds no jurisdiction of truth despite belonging to the corpus.

## Correction 2 · The three tests are ordered, not mutually exclusive

### Defect

The Framework claims the three jurisdictional tests are *"mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive."* **They are not mutually exclusive as phrased, and the claim is attackable.**

A payment is an act by a counterparty *and* an evidenced fact about something that occurred. Acceptance and rescission have the same character. Under the tests as written, both the first and second return true, and the phrasing supplies no rule for which governs.

**The more serious half of the defect, which the correction exposes.** The first test was phrased *"Is there a counterparty?"* That is too broad, and it mis-routes a real case. A venue may be a party we contract with — therefore a counterparty. But *"the Marriott's loading dock closes at four"* is an observation about a subject and belongs to **Atlas**. Under the original test it returns *counterparty: yes* and routes to **Engagement**, which is wrong.

The correction narrows the first test from the mere presence of a counterparty to a counterparty's **act upon an undertaking** — and that narrowing repairs the mis-routing, not merely the phrasing.

### Superseded text

> **Article III.1** — "Truth is partitioned by three tests that are **mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive**:
>
> > **Is there a counterparty?** → Engagement.
> > **Is it a claim about a subject?** → Atlas.
> > **Is it a thing we hold?** → Knowledge.
>
> No truth in a catering business escapes all three, and none satisfies two."

### Replacement text

> **Article III.1** — Truth is partitioned by three tests that are **ordered and jurisdictionally exclusive**. They are applied in sequence, and the first that answers governs.
>
> > **1 · Does a counterparty's act create, vary or discharge an undertaking?** → **Engagement**
> > **2 · Otherwise, is it a claim about a subject?** → **Atlas**
> > **3 · Otherwise, is it a thing we hold as approved and reusable?** → **Knowledge**
>
> **Why the order is principled rather than convenient.** Some facts are describable under more than one test. A payment is both a counterparty's act and an evidenced occurrence; so is an acceptance. The order resolves them because **constitutional significance follows the source of consequence.** What matters about a payment is not that money moved — that is merely describable — but that an obligation was discharged by a party we cannot compel. The counterparty relation is what gives the fact its consequence, so the counterparty test is asked first.
>
> The first test is deliberately narrow. It does not ask whether a counterparty is *involved*; it asks whether a counterparty's **act** creates, varies or discharges an undertaking. An observation about a venue that also happens to be a counterparty is not such an act, and correctly falls to Atlas at the second test.
>
> **On exhaustiveness.** No truth has been found that answers none of the three. That is a finding established by the attack recorded in Article VIII.1 — an empirical claim about this domain, sustained until a counterexample is produced — not a claim of logical necessity. The burden of producing such a counterexample rests on whoever asserts a new jurisdiction (VI.3).

### Consequential clarifications

**Article IV.2, Level 1** — "the resolution is the three tests, never precedence" is clarified to read "the resolution is the **ordered** tests of III.1, never precedence." The three truth constitutions remain peers; the ordering is a routing rule within Level 1, not a ranking of the documents.

**Article VI.3** and **Article VIII.1** — references to "all three tests" are read as the ordered tests. Both passages are already correctly hedged ("no truth was found that fails all three") and require no change.

**Final Verdict** — "three partition truth by tests that are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive" is replaced by "**three partition truth by ordered tests that are jurisdictionally exclusive, and exhaustive so far as attack has established.**"

---

## What this amendment does not change

**No jurisdiction moves.** Every assignment in the Article III matrix is unchanged. Payments, acceptances and rescissions were already assigned to Engagement and remain there; the correction supplies the rule that was doing the work implicitly.

**The order of interpretation is unchanged.** Article IV stands in full, including the Dependency Map at Level 2.

**No document's authority changes**, no ceremony is added or removed, and no canonical object in Atlas, Engagement or Knowledge is affected.

**The Final Verdict is unchanged in substance.** The corpus remains complete; every constitutional question retains exactly one governing authority; implementation may proceed by derivation.

**One latent defect is repaired.** The venue-observation mis-routing described above would have sent an Atlas truth to Engagement. It is corrected by the narrowing of the first test, and no other mis-routing of that class survives: Knowledge is exclusive against both of the others by construction, since doctrine has neither a subject (K‑IV.3) nor a counterparty.

## Procedural note

This amendment was adopted under the Framework's own rules — highest bar for a Framework change (VI.1), appended and dated with superseded text retained (VI.4). What was constitutional before 5 August 2026 remains knowable, as VI.4 requires.

That the corpus's first amendment was a correction of over-claim rather than a change of architecture is the outcome the amendment procedure exists to produce. **Correction 2 in particular was a genuine error in my reasoning, not merely in my wording**: I asserted a logical property the tests did not have, and the phrasing that made the claim false also concealed a routing defect. It was found by attack, corrected by citation, and cost nothing but a paragraph — which is the whole argument for having written the corpus this way.
